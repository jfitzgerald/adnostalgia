California - Where Life is Better (1928)

Subjects
Californians Inc., Brochure
Format
Printed ephemera
Collection
Ad Nostalgia archive

Image note

A smiling woman and child gather armfuls of red, white, and pink flowers beneath a monumental mountain, transforming the California landscape into a garden of abundance. Broad, painterly strokes soften the figures and scenery into a sun-warmed pastoral vision, while the deep greens and russets of the foothills lend weight to the brilliant foreground. Below, the expansive serif lettering—“California,” followed by the quietly confident promise “WHERE LIFE IS BETTER”—gives the image the authority of both destination and declaration.

Created in 1928 and associated with Californians Inc., this brochure belongs to a vigorous era of statewide promotion, when boosters used illustrated print to attract tourists, settlers, and investment. The flowering field suggests healthful outdoor living and agricultural fertility, while the fashionably bobbed woman and young child present California as modern, wholesome, and family-centered. Its unnamed illustrator worked in the tradition of romantic regionalist commercial art, favoring atmospheric color and idealized scenery over documentary detail. The likely audience—prospective visitors and middle-class households contemplating travel or relocation—was invited to imagine not simply a holiday, but a more gracious future. Left beyond the frame were the labor, water, land, and social inequalities that sustained such visions of abundance; what remains is a particularly eloquent example of California selling itself as a state of mind.
