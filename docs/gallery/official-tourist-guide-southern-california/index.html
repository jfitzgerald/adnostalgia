Image note

A compact atlas of pleasure unfolds across this lively cover: snow sports, desert riding, citrus groves, city landmarks, golf, dancing, sailing, and bathing all occupy the same improbable landscape. Above them, the hand-lettered blue script of “Southern California” sweeps across a black panel, while a yellow notice announces the All-Year Club’s visitor services. The crowded, brightly colored composition turns the region into a genial picture puzzle, its simplified figures and jaunty outlines suggesting the popular cartoon-inflected commercial illustration of the interwar era.

Issued as an official tourist guide by the All-Year Club of Southern California, the booklet promotes the organization’s central promise: varied recreation within reach in every season. Mountains, missions, palms, orange trees, desert cacti, fashionable hotels, and a busy harbor are compressed into one continuous realm of mobility and abundance. Automobiles, boats, and an ocean liner help bind these attractions together, offering visitors a modern landscape made conveniently available for touring.

The intended traveler encounters not a single destination but an entire way of life—healthy, sociable, prosperous, and perpetually outdoors. Likely distributed through tourist-information channels, the guide transforms practical assistance into regional theater. Its cheerful panorama leaves congestion, labor, and social inequality outside the frame; yet as a historical object, it vividly records how Southern California was packaged for national consumption as a place where snow, sunshine, agriculture, urban sophistication, and fantasy might coexist in a single holiday.
