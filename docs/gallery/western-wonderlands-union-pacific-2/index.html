Western Wonderlands (Union Pacific Railroad)

Subjects
Union Pacific, Western Wonderlands
Format
Printed ephemera
Collection
Ad Nostalgia archive

Image note

A jaunty cowgirl, a desert mission, palm trees, mountain ranges, horseback riders, and a sleek passenger train assemble here as a scrapbook of Western promise. Broad strokes of turquoise, coral, green, and ocher give the scenes the freshness of painted travel sketches, while the bold slab-serif title and Union Pacific shield anchor the page with unmistakable corporate authority. Rather than describing a single destination, the advertisement compresses the West—including scenery suggestive of Southern California and the desert Southwest—into an inviting sequence of romance, sunshine, and motion.

Likely dating from the mid-twentieth century, this piece reflects an era when railroads sold journeys through illustrated booklets, posters, magazine advertisements, and other travel literature. Union Pacific presents the train not merely as transportation but as the comfortable gateway to a chain of “wonderlands.” The unnamed commercial illustrator’s economical, brushy manner belongs to the modern regionalist vocabulary of postwar advertising: picturesque enough to stir longing, simplified enough to read at a glance.

The intended traveler was invited to imagine the West as healthful, spacious, and agreeably adventurous—a landscape of leisure awaiting civilized access by rail. The image’s cheerful pageant leaves labor, displacement, and the region’s complex Indigenous and Mexican histories outside the frame. What remains is a finely distilled travel dream: the modern passenger carried effortlessly toward an older, romantic West.
