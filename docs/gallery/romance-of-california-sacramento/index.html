The Romance of California Brochure (Sacramento)

Issued by Sacramento Chamber of Commerce

Subjects
Sacramento
Format
Printed ephemera
Collection
Ad Nostalgia archive

Image note

Against a deep black ground, sweeping orange and sage-green lettering announces California as a romance before the landscape even begins. Below, a framed illustration opens like a window onto the Sacramento region: a fisherman rests beneath towering conifers beside a bright stream, while a log cabin, cultivated fields, red-roofed buildings, and distant hills recede into bands of warm gold, green, and violet. The elongated composition and decorative typography give the brochure cover the character of a travel poster in miniature. A small signature is visible, but the artist cannot be identified with confidence.

Issued by the Sacramento Chamber of Commerce, this undated brochure likely belongs to the early decades of twentieth-century regional promotion, when civic organizations used illustrated publications to attract visitors, residents, and investment. Its simplified color areas and textured, print-like surface recall the romantic regionalism of commercial illustration between the 1920s and 1930s. Rather than presenting the city itself, the cover joins outdoor recreation, timber country, fertile agriculture, and settlement into a single harmonious prospect—nature made accessible, productive, and reassuringly picturesque.

The intended viewer was offered more than scenery: California appears here as a place where leisure and prosperity coexist, and where the modern traveler might recover an imagined frontier intimacy with land and water. Absent are the labor, environmental transformation, and contested histories that sustained such abundance. Yet the object remains an eloquent artifact of civic optimism, selling Sacramento through the enduring promise of escape, opportunity, and life lived beneath the open Western sky.
