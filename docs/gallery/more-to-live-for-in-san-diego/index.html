Image note

A jubilant wave turns the Pacific into a stage in this sunny invitation to San Diego. Against bands of orange-gold sky and saturated blue water, a mother in a boldly striped swimsuit beckons from the foreground while a father and child splash ashore. The broad composition, divided by a visible center fold, appears to have been designed as a brochure spread. Hand-lettered script lends warmth and informality, while the tall red capitals of “SAN DIEGO” provide the emphatic rhythm of a travel poster. The unidentified illustrator’s smoothly modeled figures, brilliant color, and simplified scenery belong to the tradition of mid-twentieth-century commercial illustration.

“There’s More to Live for in San Diego” promises more than a seaside holiday. Its paired slogans—“Where California Began” and “California at its Best”—join regional history to an ideal of modern family leisure, presenting the city as both venerable and carefree. Likely intended for prospective visitors or residents, the image appeals to aspirations for health, recreation, domestic happiness, and year-round outdoor living. Such brochures circulated through travel bureaus, hotels, transportation offices, and civic promotional campaigns, helping Southern California sell climate as a way of life. Outside this radiant frame lie the region’s more complicated histories and uneven access to its celebrated prosperity; within it, San Diego becomes an effortless realm of surf, sunshine, and familial delight.
