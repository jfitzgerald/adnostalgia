Just Look Where We're Going this Summer in Cool Southern California

Subjects
All-Year Club of California
Format
Printed ephemera
Collection
Ad Nostalgia archive

Image note

Southern California unfolds here as a jaunty scrapbook of promised pleasures. Four overlapping picture panels carry the viewer from Pacific surf and a sunlit beach picnic to pine-shadowed mountain streams, a bustling Mexican market, and an evening premiere in the “film and radio capital.” Bright bathing suits, sporty leisurewear, gleaming automobiles, and saturated reds, greens, and blues lend the page the buoyancy of mid-century commercial illustration. Above them, the sweeping red script of “Southern California” supplies a warm, handwritten flourish, while the clipped invitation—“Just look where we’re going this summer in COOL”—turns climate itself into the region’s chief attraction.

Issued by the All-Year Club of Southern California, this advertisement likely dates from the late 1930s or 1940s, when civic tourism organizations promoted the Los Angeles region nationally through magazine advertising and free travel literature. Its ingenious composition compresses coast, mountains, Hollywood nightlife, and Spanish- and Mexican-inflected marketplaces into a single itinerary, suggesting that automobile mobility placed every landscape and diversion within easy reach. The intended traveler was a prosperous white middle-class family seeking healthful recreation, novelty, and modern convenience. Like much regional promotion of the period, however, its hospitable fantasy smooths away the inequalities and contested histories underlying these picturesque scenes. More than a vacation notice, the page sells Southern California as an abundant state of mind: youthful, mobile, varied, and perpetually refreshed.
