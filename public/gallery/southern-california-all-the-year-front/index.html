Image note

A vast, midnight-blue cliff turns this brochure cover into a theater of escape. Pale seabirds wheel across a narrow band of sky while, below, an elegantly dressed woman raises one arm beside a green parasol, striped beach cushions, and a waiting yellow motorcar. The figures are tiny against the monumental coast, yet their fashionable clothes and effortless leisure make the landscape seem comfortably available. Beneath the picture, widely spaced serif capitals announce “CALIFORNIA,” balanced by the more intimate, gently italic promise “All the Year.”

Likely dating from the interwar era, the design draws upon Art Deco simplification and romantic travel illustration: flattened shapes, dramatic scale, saturated color, and crisp silhouettes replace literal topographical detail. As the front of a promotional brochure, it would have offered prospective visitors not a single documented destination but an ideal Southern California—sunlit, mobile, prosperous, and perpetually in season. The automobile signals a new freedom to tour the coast, while beach fashions, parasols, and soaring birds lend the scene notes of health, romance, and release.

Such imagery addressed travelers with the means and leisure to journey west, translating the region’s varied coastline into a modern playground. Its cultivated ease leaves labor, racial exclusion, and the uneven realities of regional growth outside the frame. What remains is the brochure’s enduring dream: California as a place where dramatic nature and stylish modern life meet beneath an apparently inexhaustible sky.
