Image note

Five towering palms frame a blue bay, white sails, clipped lawns, and strolling vacationers in this buoyant invitation to “Cool summer days and nights in San Diego.” Below, a scrapbook-like cascade of tilted snapshots expands the promise: broad beaches, Del Mar racing, an excursion to Tijuana, and deep-sea fishing. Saturated greens, yellows, and blues meet exuberant display lettering and an elegant script headline, while the San Diego California Club’s bold signature anchors the page. The uncredited artist’s stylized realism and visibly screened color give the scene the lively texture of period magazine reproduction.

Likely dating from the interwar or early postwar decades, the advertisement presents San Diego as both a restful seaside resort and a gateway to cosmopolitan adventure. Automobiles, harbor excursions, sport fishing, and a border crossing only “30 minutes away” turn mobility itself into a holiday pleasure. References to Del Mar and Agua Caliente place the city within a wider Southern California tourism landscape shaped by resort development, improved highways, and increasingly accessible leisure travel.

Probably intended for a national magazine or travel publication, the page addresses comfortable middle-class readers with the time and means to pursue scenery, sport, and social distinction. Its detachable inquiry form—for vacation information, hotels, permanent homes, and business or agricultural opportunities—quietly converts the tourist’s daydream into the possibility of relocation and investment. The image offers Southern California as an effortless realm of sunshine and abundance, leaving beyond its frame the labor and social inequalities that sustained such visions.
