Format

Collection

Image note

With arms raised beneath a garland of roses, a classically draped woman presides over Pasadena’s 1912 Tournament of Roses like a modern goddess of abundance. Her cream-and-gold gown stands against a garden dense with red, pink, and yellow blossoms, while Roman chariots race through clouds of dust behind her. Monumental lettering, architectural columns, and a glowing red border give the composition the ceremony of a theatrical proscenium. The richly layered color and emphatic outlines suggest commercial color lithography, a medium well suited to posters meant to command attention at a glance.

The advertisement presents Southern California winter as a marvelous inversion: flowers, spectacle, and outdoor festivity on New Year’s Day. Its classical imagery elevates a civic celebration into a pageant of timeless beauty, while the chariot race lends speed and excitement to the floral profusion. Such an image would have appealed to tourists and prospective residents in colder regions, offering Pasadena as a place where climate promised health, leisure, and prosperity. It also reflects an era when expanding rail travel and aggressive regional promotion helped make Southern California a nationally imagined destination.

Yet this radiant vision is selective. The unnamed artist transforms the city into an Arcadian stage, leaving labor, water, and the region’s social divisions outside the frame. What remains is the dream Pasadena wished to send abroad: cultivated, cosmopolitan, and perpetually in bloom.
