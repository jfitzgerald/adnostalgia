National Orange Show

California's Midwinter - Greatest Event - Third National Orange Show - Reduced Rates on All Railroads - San Bernardino California - Feb. 17-22, 1913

Format
Printed ephemera
Collection
Ad Nostalgia archive

Image note

A ragged, map-like opening transforms California into a window onto abundance. Across it, brilliant red lettering announces the Third National Orange Show, while a Franciscan friar stands before regimented citrus groves, cradling fruit beside a laden basket. Behind him rise a mission church, cultivated foothills, and snow-tipped mountains beneath a pale blue sky. The dark olive ground, warm yellow border, and emphatic hand-drawn type give the poster the bold clarity of early color lithography; the tiny imprint of Western Litho Co. of Los Angeles identifies its printer, though the designer remains unknown.

Held in San Bernardino, February 17–22, 1913, the exhibition presented citrus agriculture as both spectacle and civic achievement. The friar and mission architecture summon a romanticized Spanish and Mexican past, while the precisely ordered orchards proclaim modern irrigation, enterprise, and prosperity. “Reduced rates on all railroads” reveals the poster’s practical purpose: to draw visitors from across the region at a moment when rail travel was accelerating Southern California tourism and settlement. Likely displayed in stations, ticket offices, storefronts, and public gathering places, it appealed to excursionists, investors, growers, and prospective residents alike. Its pastoral vision leaves labor, water politics, and displacement outside the frame, yet eloquently preserves the era’s prevailing California dream—a fertile landscape where history, commerce, and sunshine seemed to ripen together.
