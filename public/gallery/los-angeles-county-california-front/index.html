Image note

A bold black cartouche announces “Los Angeles County” in block capitals, softened by the sweeping script of “California.” Below, an expansive coastal panorama unfolds in sun-washed turquoise, coral, ocher, and green. Bathers lounge beneath a great blue parasol; sailboats and steamships cross the harbor; red-tiled roofs, a mission-style bell tower, mountains, palms, and wheeling seabirds gather the county’s attractions into one inviting, deliberately abundant prospect.

Associated in the surviving metadata with the All-Year Club, this cover likely promoted Los Angeles County as a destination unconstrained by season. Its romantic regionalism turns geography into promise: sea bathing, scenic grandeur, modern transportation, and an apparently effortless outdoor life coexist within a single view. The juxtaposition of fashionable leisure with busy maritime traffic presents Southern California as both playground and prosperous gateway—a particularly potent image during the decades when tourism, automobile travel, aviation, and metropolitan growth were reshaping the region.

The unidentified illustrator employs crisply bounded forms, flattened areas of color, and a commanding poster-like composition suited to a tourism brochure or promotional publication. The image addresses prospective visitors and settlers alike, offering mobility, health, sociability, and escape beneath perennial sunshine. Yet its harmonious tableau is also selective: the labor, inequalities, and environmental pressures underlying Los Angeles County’s growth remain beyond the frame. What endures is the advertisement’s carefully fashioned dream—a place where mountains meet the sea and modern life seems to have taken a holiday.
