Where to Stay in Southern California

A guide to hotels, motels, and resorts in sotuhern California. A publication of the All-Year Club of Southern California's Community Travel Service Organization (Since 1921)

Image note

A brilliant field of sun-yellow unfolds beneath a sober black band, turning Southern California into a continuous panorama of possibility. Modern hotels and curving highways share the scene with beaches, palm groves, mountains, horseback riders, sailboats, and fashionably dressed couples. Even the “O” in “Southern” becomes a smiling sun, while bold, condensed lettering gives the folded brochure the confidence of a roadside sign. The restricted yellow-and-black palette and montage-like drawing suggest economical commercial printing shaped by mid-century modern design.

Published by the All-Year Club of Southern California, a regional tourism organization founded in 1921, this guide promoted hotels, motels, and resorts by presenting the region itself as the attraction. Its imagery speaks to an era of expanding automobile tourism, modern lodging, and postwar mobility, when visitors increasingly imagined Southern California as a landscape freely traversed by car. Likely distributed through travel bureaus, transportation offices, or lodging networks, the brochure addressed vacationers seeking sunshine, recreation, romance, and metropolitan sophistication in one journey. Yet its cheerful synthesis also edits the region into an effortless playground, leaving labor, segregation, congestion, and rapid development outside the frame. More than a directory, it offered a compact dream: an entire way of life, perpetually in season.
