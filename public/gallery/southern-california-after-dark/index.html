What Makes Life Worth Living?

A sight you'll always remember. The beckoning lights of Southern California after dark.

Subjects
All-Year Club of California
Format
Printed ephemera
Collection
Ad Nostalgia archive

Image note

From a shadowed hillside, a couple gazes across a vast jeweled plain of boulevards and neighborhoods, while searchlights sweep the deep blue California night. Palm silhouettes, twinkling streets, and the intimate pairing of the figures turn metropolitan Los Angeles into a landscape of romance and possibility. Below, the emphatic question “What makes life worth living?” appears in bold turquoise lettering beside a small August calendar. Lively spot illustrations of beaches, golf, swimming, and playful resort life soften the dense columns of copy, while the sunny yellow “Southern California” panel provides a bright visual destination.

Issued by the All-Year Club of Southern California, this undated advertisement presents the region not as a single resort but as an inexhaustible calendar of experiences. Its references to travel by plane, train, automobile, and bus reflect an era when expanding transportation networks made Southern California increasingly accessible to national tourists. Likely intended for a magazine audience with disposable income and paid vacation time, the advertisement promises release from routine through climate, scenery, mobility, and abundance. The romantic, painterly night view and jaunty commercial illustrations embody the optimistic language of interwar and mid-century tourism promotion. Outside its radiant frame remain the inequalities and environmental costs that accompanied metropolitan growth; within it, however, Southern California gleams as a modern promised land—close enough to reach, yet splendidly removed from everyday life.
