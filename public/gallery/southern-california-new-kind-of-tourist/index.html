A New Kind of Tourist

A new kind of tourist has 'taken' this place. Southern California

Subjects
All-Year Club of California
Format
Printed ephemera
Collection
Ad Nostalgia archive

Image note

Beneath a canopy of monumental palms, uniformed servicemen stroll through a Southern California landscape of clipped lawns, brilliant foliage, distant mountains, and deep-blue sky. The composition turns a familiar resort paradise into a wartime stage: soldiers and sailors replace conventional vacationers, while the headline’s playful claim that a new tourist has “taken” the region links military occupation with leisurely possession. Saturated greens, blues, and sunlit ochres lend the unsigned illustration the polished, painterly immediacy of 1940s magazine advertising.

Issued by the All-Year Club of Southern California in 1943, the advertisement recasts regional promotion as patriotic duty. Its text explains that servicemen trained amid the area’s varied terrain and spent their furloughs at local homes, churches, dances, and resorts. Civilian readers were asked to postpone travel, buy war bonds, and plan a postwar visit instead. The likely national magazine audience was thus invited to regard Southern California simultaneously as arsenal, training ground, and promised holiday destination.

The advertisement preserves the region’s long-cultivated dream of healthful climate, mobility, and escape, even while acknowledging wartime limits on tourism. Its gracious interracially narrow social world and immaculate landscape omit the strains of mobilization, housing shortages, and unequal access that accompanied the era. Yet that tension gives the object its historical force: paradise is not abandoned, but held in trust until peace returns.
