How a playground goes to war!

Southern California swings into action with a 5 point program

Subjects
All-Year Club of California
Format
Printed ephemera
Collection
Ad Nostalgia archive

Image note

A sunlit “playground” is recast here as an arsenal. Beneath a theatrical headline—its opening red initial adding urgency—four boldly colored scenes turn familiar Southern California attractions toward wartime purpose: desert skies fill with aircraft, shipyards launch vessels, citrus country supplies military and industrial needs, and a soundstage rallies movie and radio entertainers. Compact captions organize these images into a “5 point program,” while the final point introduces the serviceman as a new kind of visitor. The page’s vigorous commercial illustration, saturated blues, yellows, greens, and reds, and magazine-style montage combine regional romance with the brisk tempo of wartime publicity.

Produced during the Second World War, the advertisement was sponsored by the Los Angeles County Board of Supervisors and signed by the All-Year Club of Southern California, whose coupon offers transportation advice and visitor information. It likely addressed a national magazine audience already acquainted with the region as a winter resort, assuring readers that sunshine, agriculture, aviation, shipbuilding, and Hollywood now served the nation together. Yet the promise of future travel remains carefully alive: servicemen and war workers are welcomed as guests who might return after victory. The result is both civic advertisement and morale piece—an idealized portrait of Southern California in which leisure becomes productivity, tourism becomes hospitality, and the landscape of escape is enlisted in a shared national cause.
