Fun Map of Southern California

Come do all these things yourself. Look how easy and inexpensive it is.

Subjects
All-Year Club of California
Format
Printed ephemera
Collection
Ad Nostalgia archive

Image note

Southern California bursts into view as a cheerful geography of possibility. In this exuberant “Fun Map,” the region is compressed into a theatrical panorama of mountains, beaches, desert, cities, and sea. Tiny figures ski, ride horses, golf, sail, fish, swim, and sunbathe, while labeled banners locate Santa Barbara, Malibu, Los Angeles, San Diego, and other destinations. Saturated blues, ochres, reds, and greens lend the crowded scene the sparkle of a color-printed storybook. Above it all, a genial sunlike face opens its mouth to frame the title in sweeping script and emphatic capitals—a playful emblem of the climate itself.

Produced for the All-Year Club of Southern California, the advertisement presents travel as both attainable and wonderfully abundant. Its text compares journeys by airplane, train, automobile, motor stage, and steamship, placing the image in an era when expanding transportation networks were reshaping American tourism. A mail-in coupon offering a free trip planner suggests publication in a widely circulated magazine or newspaper, aimed especially at distant travelers contemplating an escape from winter. The unnamed illustrator works in the tradition of humorous pictorial maps, blending regional romanticism, cartoon incident, and promotional spectacle. Southern California appears here as a seamless playground of health, mobility, romance, and leisure; absent are the social divisions and environmental pressures underlying the region’s rapid growth. What the advertisement sold was more than an itinerary: it was the beguiling promise that every landscape—and every pleasure—might be enjoyed in a single trip.
